What an induction head is

    

      
Say the model has just read an x in the second pass.


first pass   K 7 x Q m
second pass  K 7 x ?
                 |
                 this x was seen before, and a Q came right after it. Say Q.

      
That is the whole trick: find where this character came before, read what followed, say that. It takes two heads, one stacked on the other. A head in layer 0 reads the previous character, so every position also carries what came just before it. A head in layer 1 then asks, at each position, where was the previous character the same as mine? It attends there and copies what it finds.

      
You can see both in the maps. Each row of a map is a position reading, each column a position being read, so the empty upper right is the future, which no head may read. The previous-character head draws a line just under the main diagonal. The induction head draws a parallel line 63 positions below it, in the lower half, because the earlier copy of a character is 64 back and the thing to read is one after that.

      
Layer 0 heads reach a previous-character score around 0.5. Layer 1 heads reach a prefix-matching score of 0.6 to 0.7. This is the circuit Olsson et al. described in 2022. With one layer there is nothing beneath the head to say what came before each position, and an earlier, smaller configuration with one layer showed no event in 7000 steps.

      
The circuit is also why it stumbles. It matches on the previous character only, with no memory of the characters before that. After 1800 steps, the quick brown fox. the quick br gave ox. brox. quick. The r in br has a clean match, but long prompts with repeated letters give the head several places to land, and it lands on them. abcabcabcabcab gave cabcabc, 0123456789 0123456 gave 789 0123456789 0, and qwertyuiop[] qwertyu gave iop[] qwertyuiop.

    

  


  

    
Why this is a phase change

    

      
On the first pass through a random sequence the best possible loss is chance, ln 96 = 4.56 nats, however long you train. So the difference between the first pass and the second measures copying alone. That difference is the copy advantage. It stays near zero while the model is learning nothing that helps, and then it moves all at once: 0.24, 0.36, 1.72, 2.33 across four evals, 25 steps apart, in one run.

      
The instruments come from Howe, arXiv 2609.19000. The event is copy advantage at or above 2 nats on two consecutive evals, reported at the second. The precursor is the first time a first-layer head reaches a previous-character score of 0.10. The prefix-matching score is the induction head's own signature.

    

  


  

    
What correct means here

    

      
The trainer is raw WGSL. The forward pass, the backward pass and AdamW are written by hand, with no ML library underneath. npm run parity checks the gradient of every parameter tensor against PyTorch autograd, and they agree to about 1e-6 relative L2. So what ran on your GPU is the same mathematics, not an approximation of it.

      
The configuration is 96 printable ASCII characters, 128 characters of context, width 128, 4 heads, 2 layers, batch 16. It trains only on uniform random characters made periodic.

    

  


  

    
What did not work

    

      
The synthetic bigram language from Howe's paper showed no signal in a tiny model, 8000 steps at width 64, context 64, batch 16. That is why this tab trains on random characters repeating with a period between 4 and 64, which puts all the pressure on copying.

      
A single layer does not get there. And long prompts with repeated letters still confuse it, as above, because that is what this circuit computes.

    

  


  

    
Prior work

    

      
This stands on other people's work. Sequence Toy and Piston by Vin Howe train sequence models in the browser on WebGPU. TabGPT and webgpu-llm write their training kernels by hand in WGSL. Little Language Machine trains a small transformer client-side. The circuit is from Olsson et al. 2022, In-context Learning and Induction Heads. The instruments and the transition are from Howe 2026, arXiv 2609.19000.
